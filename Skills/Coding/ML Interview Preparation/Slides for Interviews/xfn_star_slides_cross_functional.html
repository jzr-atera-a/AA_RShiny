Marketing Wanted Aggressive Targeting. Risk Wanted a Veto. ML Had No Mandate. — Navigating Four Competing Agendas to Ship a Regulated NBA Platform
XFN Navigation Competing Agendas Influence w/o Authority Proactive Alignment FCA Regulated
S
Situation — Four Competing Agendas (Chang Ch.7 Pillar 3)
I was leading delivery of the NBA ML platform with no formal authority over any of the four teams whose cooperation I needed. Marketing wanted to maximise contact volume and remove channel caps — revenue pressure. Risk & Compliance wanted model-level veto power and a 4-week MRM gate on every change. PEGA Admins owned the decisioning engine and resisted the API integration contract I proposed, preferring to own the score logic themselves. Data Science BL leads each wanted their model to take precedence in PEGA arbitration.
T
Task — The Complexity (Not a Migration. Not Reporting.)
Align four teams with directly opposing objectives around a shared governance model — while simultaneously designing the ML architecture, satisfying FCA TCF requirements, and delivering a pilot A/B test that Marketing, Risk, and the CTO would each accept as legitimate evidence. If I built the A/B platform without proactively aligning Marketing, the results would have been rejected as biased and the friction would have killed the rollout.
A
Action — Proactive Alignment Before Building Anything
1. Stakeholder mapping first: Before writing a line of code, I mapped each team's primary objective, their veto risk, and their minimum acceptable outcome — then designed the governance model around their constraints, not mine. 2. Marketing aligned before A/B design: I invited Marketing's Head of CRM to co-design the A/B test KPIs and guardrails. They defined the opt-out SLO (≤0.5%) and approved the 6-week duration — so the results were their evidence, not mine imposed on them. 3. Risk given structure, not a veto: I proposed a tiered MRM model — minor model updates (same architecture, new training data) required 3-day review; material changes 4 weeks. This gave Risk the gate they needed without blocking iteration velocity. 4. PEGA integration contract: Reframed the API as "ML provides signals, PEGA retains all decisioning authority" — PEGA Admins became advocates once they understood they kept control.
R
Result — Quantified, Cross-Functional
All four teams signed the governance charter within 8 weeks of pilot launch. Marketing co-presented the A/B results (+22% conversion, −18% opt-out) to the Group CTO — framing it as their initiative, not the ML team's. Risk approved the tiered MRM model as a bank-wide standard. PEGA Admins built the next integration themselves. Zero friction escalations post-launch.

⚡ Friction Points Navigated — Genuine Complexity
1
Marketing vs ML: Marketing initially refused to allow a 20% holdout — "we lose revenue on the control group." Resolution: I showed the cost of shipping a model the business doesn't trust — the previous 14-month stall. They accepted the holdout as insurance.
2
Risk 4-week gate: Blocking iteration entirely. Resolution: tiered MRM — minor vs material changes. Required 3 rounds of negotiation with Group CRO. Final model gave Risk more visibility, not less, so they accepted.
3
PEGA ownership conflict: PEGA Admins saw the API as a threat. Resolution: I changed the framing to "ML scores, PEGA decides" — they retained arbitration authority and became the integration's champions.
4
Wealth BL data refusal: Cited client confidentiality to block data sharing. Resolution: escalated to Group CTO with two options (proceed without Wealth data / get Data Committee approval). Chose the latter — 6-week delay but no compliance risk.

02
Innovate UK Had No ML Evidence Standard. DfT Disagreed with Engineering. Atera Engineering Wanted a Different Model. — Three-Body Alignment on a Safety-Critical Platform
XFN Navigation Regulatory Conflict Influence w/o Authority Safety-Critical AV Act 2024
S
Situation — Three External Bodies, Opposing Standards
I was the ML lead on CAM Pathfinder One with no authority over Innovate UK, DfT, or Atera's engineering team — all three of whose cooperation was required for delivery. Innovate UK (grant body) had published WP deliverables in vague English — each work package owner interpreted them differently. DfT technical reviewers wanted a safety case standard that conflicted with what Innovate UK had told us to produce. Atera's internal engineering team had proposed a single neural network end-to-end — directly contradicting the decomposed three-model architecture I believed was necessary for regulatory explainability.
T
Task — The Complexity (Three Conflicting Authorities, One Deadline)
Align Innovate UK, DfT, and internal engineering around a single ML safety case that satisfied the AV Act 2024, was evidenced by WP6 deliverables, and was technically defensible — while the three parties had never been in the same room and operated on different timelines. Failure to align DfT before the WP5 safety case submission would have triggered a grant audit. Failure to align engineering would have shipped a model I could not defend under AV Act scrutiny.
A
Action — Proactive Three-Way Alignment Before Any Code
1. Convened a joint scoping session with Innovate UK programme manager and DfT technical monitor in week 2 — before any architecture was committed to. Surfaced the standard conflict directly: "You have told us different things — we need one agreed specification before we build." This was uncomfortable but avoided a far more expensive misalignment at submission. 2. Translated the conflict into a decision: Presented a one-page comparison of the two interpretations with the regulatory risk of each — gave both parties a concrete choice, not an abstract debate. They reached agreement in the session. 3. Won internal engineering alignment through evidence, not authority: Rather than overriding the engineering team's preference, I ran a 2-week model comparison — their end-to-end NN vs my decomposed pipeline — on held-out data. The explainability gap was decisive: SHAP outputs from XGBoost satisfied WP5; the NN produced no auditable rationale. 4. Shared the DfT agreement back to all parties immediately — creating a single written record that prevented later reinterpretation.
R
Result — All Three Bodies Aligned, Safety Case Accepted
DfT accepted the SHAP + calibration diagram as safety case evidence — establishing a reusable template for future UK AV projects. Innovate UK approved all 8 WPs on schedule. Engineering team's NN approach was retained as a research branch — preserving the relationship while keeping the production architecture defensible. Atera CTO approved expansion to two additional ODDs within 6 weeks. Zero grant compliance flags across the full project lifecycle.

Engineering NN preference: Senior engineer believed decomposition was over-engineering. Resolution: 2-week model comparison on held-out data. Their preferred model produced no auditable SHAP rationale — WP5 would have failed. Evidence, not hierarchy, resolved it.

Proactively aligned the safety expert on this approach before WP3 began — not after the data gap was discovered.
4
OSM rural data quality: Discovered 15% of rural B-roads had missing speed limits — a features gap that would have caused model failures at WP6 submission. Surfaced in week 3 data audit; imputation strategy agreed with engineering and WP5 lead before modelling began.
