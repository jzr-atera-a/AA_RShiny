@OVERVIEW
This individual reflection is written in the first person as the chief executive of NVIDIA, covering the first two years after a leadership change, and is organised around the frameworks of a CEO's unique work, Porter's five forces, Helmer's seven powers and Porter and Nohria's research on how CEOs use their time. Its themes are strategic vision in the face of competition from DeepSeek, capital allocation, governance, investor confidence and personal leadership. The author presents the period as one of continuity and renewal, and concludes that purposeful, judgement-led leadership sustains growth in a disrupted market.

@BLOCK Vision, competition and talent|Setting the vision and strategy
The reflection applies Porter's five forces to re-evaluate NVIDIA's position after the rise of a Chinese competitor, and proposes to deepen defensible resources such as the CUDA software ecosystem and data-centre leadership, reduce dependence on suppliers, and build proprietary models. It credits talent density and fair pay as a source of advantage, citing a recruitment case in which a number of experienced engineers joined from a rival. The analysis is clearest where it links each strategic choice to a named source of advantage. The weakness is that the frameworks are named and mapped to decisions quickly, and the reflection rarely asks whether the frameworks explain what actually happened.

@BLOCK Capital, governance and trust with investors|Capital allocation, governance and the share-price shock
The capital section argues for investment in foundational models and data-centre offerings, and for acquisitions and equity stakes rather than large buybacks, with the aim of deepening lock-in. The governance section describes redesigned board reporting, new directors, and stricter standards on AI security and platform dependence. The most vivid passage describes a sharp fall in the share price after a competitor's announcement, and the author's decision to praise that competitor publicly and to hold a shareholder meeting to restate the strategy. The reflection presents this as a demonstration of integrity and confidence. The choice is interesting, but the reflection does not weigh the alternative of acknowledging the threat more directly, or explain how the public praise was judged by the board and investors.

@BLOCK Decision-making and the limits of the role|Personal leadership
The final section describes a structured decision model that combines stakeholder mapping, scenario planning and intuition, and reports a circle of outside advisers in AI policy, deep tech and national security. It draws on Porter and Nohria's research on how CEOs spend their time. This is the most personal section, and the most generic. It describes what a good leader does rather than what this leader did in a specific decision, and it gives little detail on trade-offs.

@CRITIQUE
The reflection is well organised and its link between each framework and each leadership action is easy to follow. Its main weakness is in the premise. The piece is written as if its author had taken over as chief executive in May 2023 after the departure of NVIDIA's founder. That is not part of NVIDIA's public record as far as I am aware, so the piece reads as a role-play. A reflection that is presented as a record of real experience needs to say so, and to separate what is the author's judgement from what is documented. Several figures are given without sources or dates, including the number of hires from a rival, the share of hires they represent, the market value lost, and the growth in data-centre revenue, and some of these are hard to check. The reflection also praises its own choices, such as the public response to the competitor, without considering any argument against them. A stronger version would make the role-play explicit, tie each decision to the evidence available at the time, and test one decision against the framework it claims to apply.

@LINKS
The reflection shows the course's leadership material in use, with an emphasis on how a leader makes strategy credible to investors and staff during a shock. Its clearest lesson is that a durable advantage rests on resources that competitors find hard to copy, and that a leader's communication is part of how those resources are protected.
