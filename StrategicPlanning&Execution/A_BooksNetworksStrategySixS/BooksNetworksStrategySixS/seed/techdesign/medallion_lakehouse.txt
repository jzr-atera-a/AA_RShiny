title: Medallion Lakehouse
variant: Bronze, silver and gold layers
summary: The medallion architecture organises a lakehouse into progressively refined layers. Bronze keeps raw data as ingested, silver holds cleaned, conformed and deduplicated data, and gold holds business-level aggregates and feature tables ready for analytics, BI and machine learning.
when_to_use: You want one governed platform for raw, curated and analytics-ready data with clear quality stages and lineage.
when_not_to_use: Data is small and simple enough for a single warehouse schema, or ultra-low-latency serving is the main need.
complexity: Medium
tags: medallion, lakehouse, bronze silver gold, delta lake, governance

@step
order: 1
text: Ingestion jobs land data from databases, files and streams.
components: C1,C2,C3,C4

@step
order: 2
text: Raw data is stored unchanged in bronze.
components: C5

@step
order: 3
text: Silver transformations clean, deduplicate and validate, with quality checks quarantining bad records.
components: C6,C10,C7

@step
order: 4
text: Gold transformations build business marts and feature tables.
components: C8,C9

@step
order: 5
text: BI and ML consume gold and silver data under catalog governance.
components: C12,C13,C11

@tradeoff
kind: pro
text: Clear quality stages, easy debugging and full reprocessing from bronze.

@tradeoff
kind: pro
text: One platform serves BI, data science and streaming.

@tradeoff
kind: con
text: Data is stored several times across layers.

@tradeoff
kind: con
text: Layer boundaries need discipline and naming standards.

@tradeoff
kind: risk
text: Without governance silver and gold can become unowned swamps.

@tradeoff
kind: cost
text: Storage and compute grow with each layer and duplicate copy.

@tradeoff
kind: scaling
text: Partition and compact tables; scale compute per layer independently.
